# Craft & Law — the transferable spine (condensed, cited)

Load this when drafting or diagnosing a Nexus asset. Three parts: the **15 transferable
principles** (craft that survives modern scrutiny), the **AU-law claim table** (the hard
constraint), and the **awareness + sophistication frameworks** (the diagnosis that comes before
a single line). Evidence tiers: `[T1]` = primary book / peer-reviewed / archive / ACCC / Google;
`[T2]` = reputable secondary (≥2 independent ≈ one T1). Round-number "results" stats from the
ancestors are **legend until an invoice/audit is shown** — do not repeat as fact.

---

## Part 1 — The 15 transferable principles

Each recurs across multiple greats *and* still holds under law + Google's trust lens. Origin is
credited honestly (Koenig, not Bernbach, wrote the VW words).

1. **Enter the conversation already in the prospect's mind.** Start at their live desire/fear/
   question, not your product. *Collier → Halbert → Schwartz.* `[T1 — Collier, The Robert Collier Letter Book, 1931]`
2. **Channel existing demand; don't manufacture it.** Pick the "starving crowd" / mass desire
   first — the market beats the wording. *Schwartz + Halbert.* `[T1 — Schwartz, Breakthrough Advertising, 1966]`
3. **Diagnose awareness + sophistication before writing** (see Part 3 — the most funnel-ready
   framework on the list). *Schwartz.* `[T1 — Schwartz, 1966]`
4. **Lead with one specific, unique, valuable proposition (USP).** One dominant benefit rivals
   can't claim. *Reeves (named it); Hopkins's pre-emptive claim anticipated it.* `[T1 — Reeves, Reality in Advertising, 1961]`
5. **The headline / first line does most of the work.** Test many; the first sentence exists only
   to earn the second (the "slippery slide"). *Caples + Ogilvy + Sugarman.* `[T1 — Caples, Tested Advertising Methods, 1932; Sugarman, The Adweek Copywriting Handbook, 2006]`
6. **Test everything; measured response, not opinion, decides.** Key-code, split-run, compare.
   *Hopkins → Caples → Bencivenga.* `[T1 — Hopkins, Scientific Advertising, 1923]`
7. **Research first, write second (~90/10).** Immerse in the product; harvest the customer's own
   vocabulary — "copy is assembled, not written." *Schwartz + Resor + Ogilvy (Gallup).* `[T1 — Schwartz, 1966; Resor via Advertising & Society Review, doi:10.1353/asr.2015.0008]`
8. **Specificity and proof beat generality and adjectives.** Concrete facts (the electric clock)
   are credible where superlatives aren't — "nobody buys without belief." *Ogilvy + Hopkins +
   Bencivenga.* `[T1 — Ogilvy, Confessions of an Advertising Man, 1963]`
9. **Sell the emotional/identity truth, not just the feature.** People buy how it makes them feel
   and who it lets them be. *Resor ("a skin you love to touch") + Polykoff + Burnett.* `[T1 — Scott, Advertising & Society Review, doi:10.1353/asr.2015.0008]`
10. **Story is the most powerful delivery vehicle.** Wrap the pitch in narrative + curiosity.
    *Caples + Sugarman + Halbert.* `[T1 — Sugarman, 2006]`
11. **Respect the reader's intelligence; leave room.** "The consumer is not a moron." Wit and
    honesty out-sell shouting. *Ogilvy + Bernbach.* `[T1 — Ogilvy, 1963]`
12. **Honesty / admitting the flaw builds trust that sells.** "Lemon"; confession-then-resolution.
    *Bernbach/Koenig + Collier.* `[T2 — Wikipedia, Think Small / Lemon, citing Koenig via The Drum]`
13. **Consistency + longevity compound.** One coherent personality or proposition held for years
    beats reinvention. *Ogilvy (brand image) + Reeves + Burnett.* `[T1 — Ogilvy on Advertising, 1983]`
14. **Get read at all — win the "A-pile."** Format and first impression earn attention before you
    can persuade. *Halbert + Sugarman.* `[T1 — Halbert, The Boron Letters]`
15. **Only advertise a product worth advertising.** Great copy accelerates a bad product's death;
    integrity is strategy. *Ogilvy (resigned accounts) + Reeves (same warning).* `[T1 — Ogilvy, 1963; Reeves, 1961]`

**Convergence:** in a sophisticated, regulated market the honest move and the high-converting move
are the *same* move — proof out-converts hype, trust is the top conversion gate, and Google's
lens rewards what the ACCC requires.

## Part 2 — The AU-law claim table (the hard constraint)

If you can't prove it today, don't publish it. Onus is on us.

| Claim type / conduct | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Any factual/advertised claim** | Must be provable — accurate, truthful, on **reasonable grounds** at the time made (incl. forward-looking projections). Keep the substantiation record on file. | `[T1 — ACCC, false or misleading claims — accc.gov.au/consumers/advertising-and-promotions/false-or-misleading-claims]` |
| **Misleading/deceptive conduct (s18 ACL)** | Intent irrelevant; judged on the **overall impression**; **silence/omission can mislead** if a material detail is withheld. | `[T1 — ACCC, as above]` `[T2 — Gilbert + Tobin, ACL]` |
| **False representations (s29 ACL)** | Bans false reps re standard, quality, grade, composition, sponsorship/approval, **testimonials, price, place of origin**. | `[T2 — Gilbert + Tobin]` `[T1 — ACCC, country-of-origin]` |
| **Penalties (body corporate)** *(SSOT for this figure)* | Per contravention, the **greatest of ~AUD $100m / 3× benefit / 30% of adjusted turnover** (Treasury Laws Amendment — passed 26 Mar 2026, applies to conduct on/after 28 Mar 2026). Individual max **confirmed unchanged at AUD $2.5m** (Ashurst). *⚠ Verified 2026-07-08; beyond base knowledge cutoff — re-confirm with ACCC each campaign.* | `[T2 — Ashurst; DW Fox Tucker ($50m→$100m)]` |
| **Superlatives** ("best/#1/cheapest/fastest/most trusted") | Not without **dated, provable** evidence. Replace adjective → number → source. | `[T1 — ACCC, false or misleading claims]` |
| **Reviews / testimonials** | Genuine, from someone who **actually used** the service; connection or **incentive prominently disclosed** (incentive applies equally to positive **and** negative); no cherry-picking/suppressing negatives. | `[T1 — ACCC, online reviews must be genuine]` |
| **"Was/now" & % off** | Only if the item genuinely sold at the "was" price for a **reasonable prior period** (Zamel's, Coles, Emma Sleep). | `[T1 — ACCC, two-price comparison advertising]` `[T2 — Gadens, ACCC v Coles]` |
| **Invented urgency/scarcity** | Illegal deceptive patterns — resetting timers, phantom stock, fake "only 2 left" (Emma Sleep, **reported ~AUD $15m** — single-source, confirm; ACCC 2026-27 priority). Only genuine, provable deadlines/availability. | `[T2 — PPC Land (Emma Sleep, single source); T1 — ACCC Boxing-Day warning]` |
| **Green / "eco" / carbon** | ACCC 8 principles — accurate, evidenced (independent preferred), **specific** (no unqualified "green/sustainable"), honest imagery; aspirational claims need timeframe + grounds. | `[T1 — ACCC greenwashing guidelines PDF]` |
| **"Australian Made" / origin** | s29 — false origin reps prohibited (extends to where materials are sourced at any stage). | `[T1 — ACCC, country-of-origin labelling]` |
| **Franchise earnings/income** | Franchising Code (new code 1 Apr 2025): earnings info optional, but if disclosed → **14 days before signing** + accuracy statement; if not disclosed → state at **item 20**. Any earnings *implication* is high-risk (Ultra Tune). | `[T1 — ACCC, Franchising Code; disclosure of financial circumstances]` `[T2 — Clayton Utz]` |
| **E-E-A-T / trust (Google)** | Named authorship + credential, first-hand **Experience**, citations, transparent Who/How/Why. **Trust is paramount** — "untrustworthy pages have low E-E-A-T no matter how expert." | `[T1 — Google Search Quality Rater Guidelines; Search Central, helpful content]` |

**Verifiable claim vs puffery:** a verifiable claim is objectively checkable ("dries to S500:2021
§… standard," "response within 60 minutes") → must be substantiated. Puffery ("we treat your home
like our own") is legal but converts less — turn puffery into a tagged, sourced claim wherever
possible. `[T1 — ACCC, substantiation]`

## Part 3 — Awareness + sophistication (diagnose before you draft)

Schwartz's two axes decide headline, hook, proof and CTA *before* any line is written.

**Five stages of customer awareness** — write to the rung the reader is on:

| Stage | Reader knows… | Lead with |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | nothing of problem/product | the problem/story; earn relevance first |
| Problem-Aware | the pain, not the fix | agitate the pain, name it precisely (PAS) |
| Solution-Aware | fixes exist, not yours | your **unique mechanism** vs alternatives |
| Product-Aware | your product, unconvinced | proof, specifics, guarantee, differentiator |
| Most-Aware | ready | the offer, price, CTA — get out of the way |

`[T1 — Schwartz, Breakthrough Advertising, 1966]` `[T2 — LeadGen Economy, Schwartz 5 stages]`

**Five levels of market sophistication** — as a market hears the same claim repeatedly, escalate:
1. plain claim (virgin market) → 2. bigger/quantified claim → 3. **new mechanism** (how it works) →
4. bigger/elaborated mechanism → 5. **identification** (align with who the reader is).
Practical rule: in a crowded trade ("we're the best plumber"), a bare superiority claim is spent —
compete on **mechanism and proof**, never louder adjectives.
`[T2 — KingEshop; B-PlanNow (Schwartz pyramid)]`

**Companion frameworks to structure the draft (scaffolds, not claim-generators):**
- **PAS** (Problem–Agitate–Solution) — pain-driven demand (water damage, emergencies).
- **AIDA** (Attention–Interest–Desire–Action) — top-of-funnel awareness.
- **4 U's** — Useful, **Urgent (real only)**, Unique, **Ultra-specific** (the anti-hype discipline).
- **JTBD** — write to the job ("make my house safe, dry and insurance-approved"), not the feature.
- **Message-match / conversion scent** — the landing headline echoes the ad that delivered the
  click (up to ~212% lift). `[T2 — Unbounce; CXL]`
