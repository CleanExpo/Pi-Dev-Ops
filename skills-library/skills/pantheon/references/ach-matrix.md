# Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH) — the verdict's spine

The verdict is built on a hypotheses × evidence matrix, not on who argued loudest.

## Method

1. List every hypothesis the seats put forward (including the null / "do nothing").
2. List every evidence item from the spine + rounds, with its Admiralty grade.
3. Fill the matrix: each cell is C (consistent), I (inconsistent), or N (neutral) —
   judged per evidence item, across ALL hypotheses, before looking at totals.
4. **Actively seek disconfirmation:** for the currently-leading hypothesis, task one
   pass (or subagent) with finding evidence AGAINST it specifically. A hypothesis
   nobody tried to kill is not a survivor; it is untested.
5. The surviving hypothesis is the one with the LEAST inconsistent evidence — not the
   most consistent (consistency is cheap; inconsistency is diagnostic).
6. Sensitivity note: name the single evidence item whose reversal would flip the
   verdict. That item becomes the verdict's kill-switch line.

## Template

| Evidence (grade) | H1 | H2 | H3 | H0 (null) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 … (B2) | C | I | N | I |

Low-graded evidence (worse than C3) may appear in the matrix but is struck through and
carries zero weight — visible, non-load-bearing.
